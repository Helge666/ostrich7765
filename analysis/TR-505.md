# TR-505

## Sound ROM

One TC531000P mask ROM, 128 KiB. Three forms of its dump are known, and
`tools/tr505_rom.py` brings each into the same sound order:

| Form | File as found | SHA-1 |
|---|---|---|
| raw: the chip as read (MAME's `tr-505_rawromdump.bin`) | `TR-505_RawRomDump.BIN` | `6441d3e7b53aff4511b23021dc854b7a5cc57689` |
| pin order: address and data lines in order | `Roland TR-505 Firmware.bin` (not firmware, despite the name) | `14e80b926aed1e78cb027752dfbbe51d9b3dcf4d` |
| sound order: every sound contiguous (canonical) | `TR-505_RawRomDump_Decoded.BIN` | `70f622f07fd94bcbfdb731f303ab4e8df845469f` |

- **ROM:** raw to pin order is an exact permutation of the 8 data and 17 address lines
  (`DATA_BITS`, `ADDR_BITS` in the tool). Found from per-bit flip signatures (the sum of
  |x[a] - x[a ^ bit]| is the same for a bit and its image), then checked byte for byte.
- **ROM:** pin order to sound order is not a permutation of address lines but depends on
  the slot (8 KiB): slots 0-3 one sound each (A0 the other way round), slots 4-7 two sounds
  each interleaved even/odd, slots 8-11 one 32 KiB sound with the slot number counting
  fastest, slots 12-13 one 16 KiB sound likewise, slots 14 and 15 one sound each: 16
  sounds. Checked byte for byte against the decoded dump.
- **ROM:** samples are 8 bit, unsigned, centre 0x80. The owner listened to all 16 at
  25 kHz: they sound right.
## Measured on the owner's unit (2026-11-04)

Setup: MIDI through the Behringer XR18's MIDI out, audio into its inputs 7/8 (0 dB gain),
recorded at 48 kHz / 16 bit; scripts in `tools/measure/`.

- **measured: note map.** On channel 10, exactly 16 notes sound, each a different ROM
  sound (matched by cross-correlation of the recording with the resampled ROM sound,
  0.77-0.99 for the tonal sounds; 0.18-0.49 for the noise sounds, but each still a unique
  best match):

  | Note | ROM sound | Instrument | | Note | ROM sound | Instrument |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 35 | 4 | bass drum | | 49 | 12 (32 KiB) | crash |
  | 37 | 10 | rim shot | | 51 | 13 (16 KiB) | ride |
  | 38 | 6 | snare drum | | 62 | 7 | high conga |
  | 39 | 8 | hand clap | | 63 | 5 | low conga |
  | 41 | 1 | low tom | | 65 | 0 | timbale |
  | 42 | 15 | closed hi-hat | | 67 | 11 | high cowbell |
  | 45 | 3 | mid tom | | 68 | 9 | low cowbell |
  | 46 | 14 | open hi-hat | | 48 | 2 | high tom |

  Notes 62-68 named by the owner by ear. **documented:** the default note map in the
  TR-505 manual (Roland, Feb 1986, p. 40, as tabulated in Zoe Blade's "Roland TR MIDI
  map") is the same, note for note and name for name. The manual lets every voice's note
  be changed (25-99) and says the box starts in omni mode, channel 10.
- **measured: sample rate.** Every sound but the bass drum plays at 24.94-24.97 kHz
  against the XR18's clock, i.e. 25 kHz nominal (0.2 % off: the two crystals). The bass
  drum matches best at 25.28-25.33 kHz (+1.4 %), in several windows and recordings. Open:
  a real difference, or the bass drum's filter skewing the fit.
- **measured: velocity.** Two neighbouring velocities always give the same level: the
  box uses velocity / 2, 64 steps. Level (RMS of the first 60 ms, bass drum, relative to
  velocity 127) per step 0-63:

  0.191 0.237 0.280 0.322 0.364 0.402 0.439 0.475 0.508 0.538 0.564 0.590 0.612 0.631
  0.652 0.669 0.687 0.704 0.717 0.733 0.747 0.760 0.774 0.786 0.800 0.812 0.822 0.834
  0.845 0.855 0.867 0.874 0.884 0.893 0.901 0.909 0.917 0.925 0.932 0.939 0.947 0.954
  0.960 0.966 0.972 0.978 0.984 0.989 0.995 1.000

  Steps 0-9 (velocity 1-19) give the same floor (-37 dB); from step 10 the level rises
  about linearly, then flattens. A coarse sweep of snare, rim shot and ride follows the
  same curve within about 1 dB.
- **measured: timing.** 300 bass-drum notes at random intervals (250-270 ms): the delay
  from sending to the onset spreads over 1.7 ms (standard deviation 0.36 ms), including
  the PC's USB MIDI. No service period of 0.3-12 ms shows in the delays (the weak best scores are the same
  for the TR-626, so they come from the random send pattern). So the TR-505
  reacts much faster than the TR-707/727's 2.8 ms grid (TR-727.md); its own share of the
  1.7 ms is not separable from the USB MIDI's without a reference.
- **measured:** a note off has no audible effect: every hit was followed by a note off
  after 50 ms, and the recordings match the ROM sounds well beyond that (200 ms windows).
- **Open:** the bass drum's rate. **derived (2026-11-04):** the TR-707 engine's bass
  drum, played at exactly 25 kHz, fits at +0.4 % through its band pass alone (TR-707.md);
  the +1.4 % here may be the same kind of skew.

## Firmware

The CPU is an HD6301Y0 with its program in an internal mask ROM (MAME,
`roland_tr505.cpp`); no dump is known. Note map, accent and timing must be **measured**.
