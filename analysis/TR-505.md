# TR-505

## Sound ROM

One TC531000P mask ROM, 128 KiB. Three forms of its dump are known, and
`tools/tr505_rom.py` brings each into the same sound order:

| Form | File as found | SHA-1 |
|---|---|---|
| raw: the chip as read (MAME's `tr-505_rawromdump.bin`) | `TR-505_RawRomDump.BIN` | `6441d3e7b53aff4511b23021dc854b7a5cc57689` |
| pin order: address and data lines in order | `Roland TR-505 Firmware.bin` (not firmware, despite the name) | `14e80b926aed1e78cb027752dfbbe51d9b3dcf4d` |
| sound order: every sound contiguous (canonical) | `TR-505_RawRomDump_Decoded.BIN` | `70f622f07fd94bcbfdb731f303ab4e8df845469f` |

- **ROM:** raw to pin order is an exact permutation of the 8 data and 17 address lines
  (`DATA_BITS`, `ADDR_BITS` in the tool). Found from per-bit flip signatures (the sum of
  |x[a] - x[a ^ bit]| is the same for a bit and its image), then checked byte for byte.
- **ROM:** pin order to sound order is not a permutation of address lines but depends on
  the slot (8 KiB): slots 0-3 one sound each (A0 the other way round), slots 4-7 two sounds
  each interleaved even/odd, slots 8-11 one 32 KiB sound with the slot number counting
  fastest, slots 12-13 one 16 KiB sound likewise, slots 14 and 15 one sound each: 16
  sounds. Checked byte for byte against the decoded dump.
- **ROM:** samples are 8 bit, unsigned, centre 0x80. The owner listened to all 16 at
  25 kHz: they sound right. *(Partly obsolete, 2026-11-04: in the order the unit plays
  them the hi-hats and cymbals differ; see "Sample order as the unit plays it" below.
  By ear alone noise sounds pass either way.)*

## Sample order as the unit plays it (2026-11-04)

- **measured:** against the recordings of the owner's unit (`tools/measure/coherence.py`:
  the ROM sound held at 25 kHz, rate and lag fitted, magnitude-squared coherence per
  third octave) rim shot, hand clap, toms, timbale and cowbells follow the sound order
  sample for sample up to 20 kHz (coherence 0.95-1.00). The hi-hats, crash and ride do
  not: 0.0-0.3 above 2 kHz. The hi-hats' pattern (high at low frequencies, near 0 around
  6 kHz, high again at 10-13 kHz) is that of neighbouring samples swapped.
- **measured:** the unit reads sample n of these sounds at n ^ x of the sound order,
  with these x the coherence is 0.97-0.99 up to 20 kHz (correlation 0.94-0.99, before
  0.09-0.40):

  | Sound | x | in pin order |
  |---|---|---|
  | 14, 15 open / closed hi-hat | 1 | A0 inverted (as already for slots 0-3) |
  | 13 ride | 3 | A0 and A13 inverted |
  | 12 crash | 7 | A0, A13 and A14 inverted |

  The other sounds: every permutation of the low three index bits, with bit 0 inverted or
  not, is worse than the sound order (`coherence.py --variants`; for bass drum and snare
  also every choice of the bit that selects one of the two interleaved sounds). The
  4 KiB sounds take nothing from their slot partner (least squares: under 2 %).
- **documented (TR-505 service notes, Roland, Jun 1986, Table 1 and text):** the
  MB63H114 address counter, its 8 channels selected in turn every 40 µs. The 8 KiB sounds
  step every 40 µs; the 4 KiB sounds every 20 µs (clock B), so every selection sees every
  other address; the ride's counter steps every 80 µs (clock D) and the crash's every
  160 µs (1/2 D), with D on A13 and 1/2 D on A14, the counter shifted up by one or two
  bits. **derived:** this is the sound order of the decoded dump; the inversions above
  are where the dump's decoding and the unit differ (the phase of D and 1/2 D at the
  selection, the counter's A0).
- `PcmMachine.cpp` (`kTr505Flip`) plays the sounds in this order; the sound order and its
  SHA-1 stay as they are. `tools/measure/response.py` (`FLIP`) and
  `tools/check_pcm_render.py` use the same table.
- **measured, after the fix** (`response.py`, relative to each sound's 1 kHz): the
  hi-hats are flat within 1 dB to 6 kHz (the "dip at 1.5-4 kHz" measured before was the
  wrong order); crash 4-8 dB and ride 2-4 dB below the others; bass drum and snare still
  8-17 dB more at 250-500 Hz than the ROM gives, which the ROM data does not contain
  (coherence there near 0): to be modelled with the analogue stages (below).
- **Open:** the same for the TR-626 (no recordings yet).

## Voicing board (documented: TR-505 service notes, Roland, Jun 1986)

- One DAC (resistor ladder R56, biased by R46/R47 220k to 5 V, buffered by IC12b
  M5218L) into a 4051 (IC13), one hold capacitor per channel (C28-C35, 3.3 nF).
- Channel groups (Table 1): the sounds of one channel cannot sound together. 0: OH, CH;
  1: ride, crash; 2: LT, MT, HT, timbale; 3: BD; 4: LCG, HCG; 5: SD; 6: LCB, HCB; 7: HCP,
  RIM. *(Replaces the assumption "the closed hi-hat cuts the open one" below; the engine
  plays these groups since 2026-11-04.)*
- Per channel: series resistor from the hold capacitor into 2.2k to the bias bus
  (R81/R82 2.2k from +5 V, C37 220 µF): HCP/RIM 100k, cowbells 82k, SD 33k, congas, BD,
  toms, cymbals, hats 47k. The node drives the base of a 2SD1469R (Q6-Q13), which gets the
  envelope through 2.2M (R59, R62, R65, R68, R71, R74, R77, R80).
- Envelopes: a second 4051 (IC14) puts the level CV (2.5-5 V) on one capacitor per
  channel (C20-C27: 0.1, 0.1, 0.047 and five 0.022 µF); the plots show peaks up to 3.1 V
  decaying to 0 V. "Decay time control" switches R83 2.2M via a 4066 (IC17a) for CH
  (short) against OH (long).
- Collectors into transimpedance amplifiers (M5218L IC15, IC16; 47k ‖ 47 pF, so about
  72 kHz): BD, SD, congas, cowbells and HCP/RIM together on IC16b, CH/OH on IC16a,
  crash/ride on IC15b; the toms on a path of their own (not yet read). Pan resistors into the two outputs (8.2k-47k); a 4066 (IC17b-d) switches the toms' pan.
- Output amplifier IC18 (volume VR2 100k ‖ 47 pF in its feedback), then 1k with 3.3 nF to
  ground (about 48 kHz) to the jacks R/MONO and L. No filter in the audio band after the
  DAC: **derived**, matching the measured response (flat within 1 dB to 10 kHz).
## Measured on the owner's unit (2026-11-04)

Setup: MIDI through the Behringer XR18's MIDI out, audio into its inputs 7/8 (0 dB gain),
recorded at 48 kHz / 16 bit; scripts in `tools/measure/`.

- **measured: note map.** On channel 10, exactly 16 notes sound, each a different ROM
  sound (matched by cross-correlation of the recording with the resampled ROM sound,
  0.77-0.99 for the tonal sounds; 0.18-0.49 for the noise sounds, but each still a unique
  best match; *the low values were the hi-hats' and cymbals' sample order, see above*):

  | Note | ROM sound | Instrument | | Note | ROM sound | Instrument |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 35 | 4 | bass drum | | 49 | 12 (32 KiB) | crash |
  | 37 | 10 | rim shot | | 51 | 13 (16 KiB) | ride |
  | 38 | 6 | snare drum | | 62 | 7 | high conga |
  | 39 | 8 | hand clap | | 63 | 5 | low conga |
  | 41 | 1 | low tom | | 65 | 0 | timbale |
  | 42 | 15 | closed hi-hat | | 67 | 11 | high cowbell |
  | 45 | 3 | mid tom | | 68 | 9 | low cowbell |
  | 46 | 14 | open hi-hat | | 48 | 2 | high tom |

  Notes 62-68 named by the owner by ear. **documented:** the default note map in the
  TR-505 manual (Roland, Feb 1986, p. 40, as tabulated in Zoe Blade's "Roland TR MIDI
  map") is the same, note for note and name for name. The manual lets every voice's note
  be changed (25-99) and says the box starts in omni mode, channel 10.
- **measured: sample rate.** Every sound but the bass drum plays at 24.94-24.97 kHz
  against the XR18's clock, i.e. 25 kHz nominal (0.2 % off: the two crystals). The bass
  drum matches best at 25.28-25.33 kHz (+1.4 %), in several windows and recordings. Open:
  a real difference, or the bass drum's filter skewing the fit.
- **measured: velocity.** Two neighbouring velocities always give the same level: the

  velocity 127) per step 0-63:

  0.191 0.237 0.280 0.322 0.364 0.402 0.439 0.475 0.508 0.538 0.564 0.590 0.612 0.631
  0.652 0.669 0.687 0.704 0.717 0.733 0.747 0.760 0.774 0.786 0.800 0.812 0.822 0.834
  0.845 0.855 0.867 0.874 0.884 0.893 0.901 0.909 0.917 0.925 0.932 0.939 0.947 0.954
  0.960 0.966 0.972 0.978 0.984 0.989 0.995 1.000

  Steps 0-9 (velocity 1-19) give the same floor (-37 dB); from step 10 the level rises
  about linearly, then flattens. A coarse sweep of snare, rim shot and ride follows the
  same curve within about 1 dB.
- **measured: timing.** 300 bass-drum notes at random intervals (250-270 ms): the delay
  from sending to the onset spreads over 1.7 ms (standard deviation 0.36 ms), including
  the PC's USB MIDI. No service period of 0.3-12 ms shows in the delays (the weak best scores are the same
  for the TR-626, so they come from the random send pattern). So the TR-505
  reacts much faster than the TR-707/727's 2.8 ms grid (TR-727.md); its own share of the
  1.7 ms is not separable from the USB MIDI's without a reference.
- **measured:** a note off has no audible effect: every hit was followed by a note off
  after 50 ms, and the recordings match the ROM sounds well beyond that (200 ms windows).
- **Open:** the bass drum's rate. **derived (2026-11-04):** the TR-707 engine's bass
  drum, played at exactly 25 kHz, fits at +0.4 % through its band pass alone (TR-707.md);
  the +1.4 % here may be the same kind of skew.

## The engine (Phase 2)

`plugin/ostrich/engine/PcmMachine.cpp`, shared with the TR-626: any of the three dump
forms brought into sound order (`SoundOrder.cpp`, the same SHA-1 as `tools/tr505_rom.py`
gives), every sound at 25 kHz, held as the DAC holds it, scaled by the measured accent
table; the note map above; a note off does nothing. Each sound has a voice of its own and
restarts when played again.

- **derived, checked against the ROM (2026-11-04):** `ostrich_engine_render 505` and
  `tools/check_pcm_render.py`: all 16 sounds at 25 kHz (within the sweep's 0.05 % step),
  correlation 0.95-0.9999 over the first 100 ms.
- *(Superseded by the channel groups of the service notes, above.)* **assumed, to measure:** the closed hi-hat cuts the open one off (usual on Roland's
  drum machines). Also to measure: whether the two sounds interleaved in one ROM slot
  (bass drum / low conga, snare / high conga, hand clap / low cowbell, rim shot / high
  cowbell) share a voice; panning and the analogue stages (Phase 3). The mix is mono for
  now. *(The voice question is answered by the channel groups: the interleaved pairs are
  on different channels and sound together.)*

## Envelopes (2026-11-04)

- **measured** (`tools/measure/envelope.py`: recording RMS over ROM RMS in 10 ms frames,
  noise subtracted, the recordings at velocity 127): every sound gets quieter than its
  ROM data along the sound; the crash by 21 dB after 850 ms, the toms by 13 dB after
  300 ms, the 4 KiB sounds by 13-25 dB after 150 ms. The fall steepens in dB: no pure
  exponential.
- **measured, fitted:** flat for a hold of 35 ms, then g = (exp(-t / tau) - v0) /
  (1 - v0) with v0 = 0.18 for all sounds (silent from g = 0); with tau per sound the fit
  is within 0.2-0.8 dB rms (a pure exponential: 0.2-1.2 dB). tau: toms and timbale
  0.26 s; congas, cowbells, clap, rim, snare 0.10-0.135 s; crash 0.58, ride 0.61, open
  hi-hat 0.64, closed hi-hat 0.21 s. The bass drum is too short to fit.
- **derived:** the three groups of tau go with the documented envelope capacitors
  (0.022, 0.047, 0.1 µF) at about 5 MOhm: the 4 KiB channels on 0.022 µF, the toms on
  0.047 µF, cymbals and hi-hats on 0.1 µF, the closed hi-hat shortened by the decay time
  control (R83). The threshold is the VCA transistor starting to conduct. The bass drum
  takes the other 4 KiB sounds' tau (0.112 s).
- The engine plays this envelope per sound, and the channel groups (above): a sound cuts
  off the other sounds of its channel. **checked** (render against recordings, level per
  10 ms frame): within about 1 dB over each sound, the crash to 800 ms; the bass drum
  falls 2.5 dB too fast at 50 ms (it measures with no decay over its 75 ms; possibly the
  VCA compressing its loud start, for the analogue stage).
- **Open, to measure:** the envelope at lower velocities. The circuit suggests that the
  accent sets the capacitor's starting voltage, so that soft notes would also fade
  sooner; the engine scales the level only.

## Levels and pan (2026-11-04)

- **measured** (`tools/measure/levels.py`: each output's RMS over the ROM sound's, first
  30 ms, inside the envelope's flat start, noise subtracted; the recorder's first channel
  taken as L, its two inputs linked as a stereo pair), dB relative to the loudest output:

  | Sound | L | R | | Sound | L | R |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | bass drum | -2.1 | -2.5 | | crash | -8.8 | -6.3 |
  | snare | 0.0 | -0.8 | | ride | -3.4 | -7.1 |
  | low tom | -1.2 | -6.1 | | open hi-hat | -10.8 | -4.0 |
  | mid tom | -1.2 | -1.0 | | closed hi-hat | -11.0 | -4.3 |
  | high tom | -6.3 | -1.5 | | hand clap | -6.2 | -6.0 |
  | timbale | -6.6 | -6.3 | | rim shot | -5.9 | -5.6 |
  | low conga | -1.5 | -1.7 | | low cowbell | -4.7 | -4.4 |
  | high conga | -1.3 | -1.5 | | high cowbell | -4.8 | -4.5 |

  R as recorded less 1.23 dB: the sounds on the centre bus (below) all read 0.5-1.5 dB
  higher on R (median 1.23).
- **documented** (service notes): BD, SD, congas, cowbells, clap and rim share one
  summing amplifier (IC16b), which feeds L and R through equal resistors (R102, R103,
  15k): centre. **derived:** the 1.23 dB is the owner's unit, most likely the two gangs of
  the volume pot (VR2, 100k dual) not tracking at the volume used; the table and the
  engine (`kTr505Out`) are the nominal, centred unit. To check: the same at full volume.
- **derived, plausibility:** within the centre bus the levels follow the documented series
  resistors roughly (SD 33k loudest; cowbells 82k and clap / rim 100k 2-4 dB lower), the
  ROM data's own levels aside.
- **checked:** the stereo render matches the recordings' levels of both outputs within
  0.1 dB (the same measurement, so this checks the engine's use of it).
- Counted in: what the analogue stages add (bass drum and snare, 250-500 Hz) is part of
  their measured level.

## Firmware

The CPU is an HD6301Y0 with its program in an internal mask ROM (MAME,
`roland_tr505.cpp`); no dump is known. Note map, accent and timing must be **measured**.
