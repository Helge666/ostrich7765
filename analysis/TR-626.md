# TR-626

## Sound ROM

One 256 KiB image is known (SHA-1 `d7891edecc68a0652d2cad82a637430ee8568818`).
`tools/tr626_rom.py` brings it into sound order (SHA-1 of the result
`2f807c433570e738b3db15c731e302237789f2d0`) and splits it into 32 sounds.
**Obsolete (2026-11-04):** that was before slot 7 turned out to hold one sound (below);
the sound order now has SHA-1 `b61f9ceae9c8570c48ce6d53ead6d5ee1bfc187b` and 30 sounds.

- **ROM:** samples are 8 bit, unsigned, centre 0x80, as on the TR-505 and TR-707.
- **ROM:** the dump has address lines **A6 and A8 swapped**. Read as it is, every sound
  jumps at the edges of 64-byte blocks (7 to 24 times the usual step between neighbouring
  samples at the A6, A7 and A8 block edges, about 1 at every other edge). Of the 48 ways to
  reorder those three bits, swapping A6 and A8 alone makes every edge smooth. The owner
  heard the dump as it is ("recognisable, but all digitally distorted") and after the swap
  ("clean").
- **ROM, measured:** with A6/A8 undone the image is two 128 KiB banks, each laid out
  almost as the TR-505's sound ROM in pin order (TR-505.md): slots 0-3 need no A0 flip,
  and slot 7 holds one sound, not two:

  | Slots (8 KiB) | Contents | Sounds |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0-3 | one sound each | 4 x 8 KiB |
  | 4-6 | two sounds each, interleaved (even / odd bytes) | 6 x 4 KiB |
  | 7 | one sound (open hi-hat in bank 0, closed hi-hat in bank 1) | 8 KiB |
  | 8-11 | one sound across four slots, the slot number counting fastest | 32 KiB |
  | 12-13 | one sound across two slots, the slot number counting fastest | 16 KiB |
  | 14, 15 | one sound each | 2 x 8 KiB |

- **ROM:** the TR-505 and TR-626 share sounds byte for byte: TR-626 slot 2 equals TR-505
  sound 2, and the even bytes of TR-626 slot 4 equal those of TR-505 pin-order slot 6.
  These confirm the A6/A8 swap and the layout of those slots.
- **measured:** that makes 15 sounds per bank, 30 in all, and the real unit plays exactly
  30 notes, each one a different ROM sound. Slot 7 shows it: as two interleaved sounds the
  hi-hats match only at half the rate (12.5 kHz) and at half the correlation; as one sound
  at 25 kHz, 0.91-0.92. At the exact rate even the noise sounds match the recordings at
  0.87-0.95, which confirms the four-slot (crash) and two-slot (ride) orders as well.

## The engine (Phase 2)

`plugin/ostrich/engine/PcmMachine.cpp`, shared with the TR-505 (TR-505.md): the sound
order of `tools/tr626_rom.py` (`SoundOrder.cpp`, same SHA-1), every sound at 25 kHz, held
as the DAC holds it, scaled by the measured accent table; the note map above; a note off
does nothing. Each sound has a voice of its own and restarts when played again.

- **derived, checked against the ROM (2026-11-04):** `ostrich_engine_render 626` and
  `tools/check_pcm_render.py`: all 30 sounds at 25 kHz (within the sweep's 0.05 % step),
  correlation 0.89-0.9999 over the first 100 ms (lowest: ride, noise).
- *(Superseded by the measured channels, below: the hi-hats do share one, and so do the
  two bass drums and the toms of both banks, but the channels are wider than that. The
  mix is stereo now.)* **assumed, to measure:** the closed hi-hat cuts the open one off (usual on Roland's
  drum machines). Not yet modelled, also to measure: whether the two sounds at the same
  place in the two banks (bass drum 1/2, low, mid, high tom 1/2, the hi-hats) share a
  voice and cut each other, as on the TR-707; tuning (the unit has a pitch per
  instrument); panning and the analogue stages (Phase 3). The mix is mono for now.

## What the unit does with its sounds (measured, 2026-11-04)

The same measurements as for the TR-505 (TR-505.md, "Sample order as the unit plays it"
to "The centre bus limits"), on the 30 single notes at velocity 127 of the note map above
and a new pair scan. No service notes or schematic of the TR-626 are at hand, so nothing
here is documented; where the TR-505's circuit is taken over, it says so.

- **measured: sample order.** Unlike the TR-505, the unit plays every sound in the sound
  order as it is: `tools/measure/coherence.py` gives 0.9-1.0 up to 20 kHz for every sound
  with energy there, hi-hats and cymbals included; no index flip is needed. (The cut of
  note 69 started after the sound's own onset; cut again from the scan with more room
  before it, it agrees as well.)
- **measured: envelopes.** The TR-505's model fits (`tools/measure/env_fit.py`): flat for
  30 ms, then g = (exp(-t / tau) - 0.12) / 0.88, silent from g = 0, one hold and one v0
  for all sounds and tau per sound, 0.2-1.1 dB rms (mean 0.49). The TR-505's values
  (35 ms, 0.18) fit nearly as well (0.52), so the circuit is likely the same. tau falls
  into the TR-505's groups: about 105 ms (bass drums, clap, cowbell, claves, tambourine,
  snare 3, mute conga, agogos, shaker), 260 ms (toms, snares 1-2, timbales, open high and
  low conga, closed hi-hat), 590 ms (crash, ride, china, cup), and 850 ms for the open
  hi-hat. The rim shot ends before the fit can tell (1.06 s over 95 ms); the render's band
  shares (below) confirm a long one: 3.8 dB rms at 100 ms, 1.05 at 1 s.
- **measured: channels.** `tools/measure/pairscan.py` played all 435 pairs of the 30 notes,
  the second 30 ms after the first; `tools/measure/cut_eval.py` fits the scan with both
  single recordings. 55 pairs cut the first sound off (what is left of it under 0.05), all
  others play on unchanged (0.9-1.1). They form exactly eight groups, every pair within a
  group cutting: as the MB63H114's eight channels on the TR-505. Our numbering (as the
  TR-505's where the sounds match):

  | Channel | Sounds (notes) |
  |---|---|
  | 0 | closed, open hi-hat (42, 46) |
  | 1 | crash, ride, china, cup (49, 51, 52, 53) |
  | 2 | all six toms, open high conga, low conga (41, 43, 45, 47, 48, 50, 63, 64) |
  | 3 | bass drum 1, 2 (35, 36) |
  | 4 | hand clap, mute high conga, shaker, claves (39, 62, 69, 75) |
  | 5 | snare drum 1, 2, high and low timbale (38, 40, 65, 66) |
  | 6 | tambourine, cowbell, high and low agogo (54, 56, 67, 68) |
  | 7 | rim shot, snare drum 3 (37, 58) |

  Within a channel tau is about the same (channel 2: 245-318 ms, 3: 110-138 ms; as the
  envelope capacitor per channel on the TR-505), except on channels 0 and 7, which have two envelopes each.
- **measured: levels and pan** (`tools/measure/levels.py`, first 30 ms against the ROM
  data). Centred: everything but these: low toms 1, 2 left (R - L -6.0 dB), high toms
  right (+6.6), hi-hats right (+8.4), crash and china right (+3.6), ride and cup left
  (-4.6). The mid toms are centred. The right output read 0.28 dB high on all centred
  sounds (0.21-0.43; the TR-505: 1.2 dB, put down to its volume pot); taken out. Loudest:
  bass drum 1, then bass drum 2 (-0.1 dB) and the rim shot (-4.5).
- **measured: envelope pulse.** As on the TR-505 the recordings have more low frequencies
  than the ROM data, and a term following the envelope explains them
  (`tools/measure/vca_fit.py`): one value for all sounds, 0.06 of full scale (the TR-505:
  0.05 at 47k), brings the band shares from 2.4 to 1.0 dB rms on average. No series
  resistors are known; all taken as the TR-505's 47k (derived). An exponential drive adds
  nothing worth having.
- **measured: no limit seen.** The TR-505's centre bus limits its loud sounds; here
  nothing shows it at single hits (bass drum 1 within 0.7 dB without), and in the pair
  scan the sounds of different channels add unchanged (0.9-1.1), the bass drums with the
  snares too. Not modelled; two loud sounds at the same instant are not yet measured.
- **derived:** the output's coupling high pass, 0.34 Hz, as the TR-505's.

`ostrich_engine_render 626` with all this against the recordings
(`tools/measure/check_render.py 626 ... --r-offset 0.28`): band shares 40 Hz-5 kHz
1.00 dB rms on average (before: 3.17), levels of every sound at both outputs within
0.6 dB (before: up to 20 dB, mono and without levels). Largest remaining: tambourine,
shaker, agogos, clap (1.6-2.5 dB), the bright sounds. Only velocity 127 is measured.
