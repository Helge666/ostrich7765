# TR-707

## Sound ROMs

| Chip | Contents | SHA-1 |
|---|---|---|
| IC34 | voices: BD, SD, LT, MT | `fd2bfe67d4d03d2b2134aa7feebe9167c44b1f8d` |
| IC35 | voices: HT, HH, RS/CB, HCP/TAMB | `5cc866f345906d817147ae2a61bc36d7be926511` |
| IC19 | crash | `965e23ad71e1f95d56307fa67272725dff46ba67` |
| IC22 | ride | `6c7c0f002ed66e4ccf182a4538d9bb239623ac43` |

All four are 32 KiB and match MAME's `tr707` set.

- **documented** (MAME `roland_tr707.cpp`, after the service notes; Chris Kann's notes):
  IC34 and IC35 hold four 8 KiB voice slots each, read by the eight counters of the
  MB63H114 address counter (1.6 MHz master clock, outputs 100 / 50 / 25 / 12.5 kHz).
  Slots 0, 1, 6 and 7 hold two sounds interleaved even/odd (BD1/BD2, SD1/SD2, RS/CB,
  HCP/TAMB).
- **documented:** counter clocks: 50 kHz for the interleaved slots (25 kHz per sound),
  25 kHz for HH, 12.5 kHz for the toms. Crash and ride run at 25 kHz with a 15-bit counter
  that stops at 0x8000; their DACs are 6 bit (D7-D2).
- **ROM:** `tools/tr707_rom.py` splits the ROMs that way. The owner listened to all 14
  sounds at those rates: they sound right.
- **documented** (MAME): 14 samples make 15 sounds: the closed and the open hi-hat play
  the same sample (HH); only the hat's envelope differs (the closed hat's capacitor
  discharges faster, through R123).
- **listened (2026-11-04):** the owner heard all 15 sounds of the engine's render
  (`ostrich_engine_render`, 48 kHz, velocities 127/64/24): all correct.
- **documented:** after the DACs: accent through a 6-bit DAC, RC envelope generators, an
  envelope / VCA for the hats, a band-pass per voice, a 15.9 kHz low-pass, pan resistors
  and an output band-pass, with component values (MAME). Phase 3.

## The engine (Phase 2)

`plugin/ostrich/engine/Tr707.cpp` is a port of MAME's `tr707_audio_device` (BSD-3-Clause,
AJR and m1macrophage): the MB63H114 at 200 kHz, the multiplying DAC with the envelopes as
its reference, the shared charge resistor R79, the hat EG and VCA, the cymbals' 6-bit DACs,
the band passes, level, low pass, pan and output band pass. The CPU side follows the
TR-727's firmware (TR-727.md): accent = velocity / 2, a 1.4 ms trigger pulse, sounds that
share a voice cut each other. Departures from MAME, each derived: the accent follower is
clamped at 0 V (MAME lets it go negative), and the DC block on the summing amps is a real
0.05 Hz RC high pass (MAME's one-pole high pass does not remove DC).

- **derived, checked against the ROM (2026-11-04):** `plugin/tools/ostrich_engine_render`
  renders every sound; `tools/check_tr707_render.py` fits the ROM sound to each mixer
  channel. All 15 play at their nominal rate (25 or 12.5 kHz, 0.00 %) with correlations of
  0.94-0.998 over the first 120 ms (the closed hi-hat 0.57: its envelope ends within the
  window; the open hi-hat, the same sample, 0.92). Lengths follow the ROM: toms 652 ms,
  open hi-hat 328 ms, crash and ride 1.28-1.31 s; the cymbals start 1.4 ms late, at the end
  of the trigger pulse (as MAME). Velocity 64 is -7 dB, 24 is -22 dB below 127.
- **derived:** the bass drums fit best at **+0.4 %** although the engine plays them at
  exactly 25 kHz: the bass drum's band pass alone skews the rate fit. The TR-505 and
  TR-626 bass drums measured +1.4 % (TR-505.md, TR-626.md) may be the same effect of their
  own filters; to be checked once those filters are modelled (Phase 3).

## Against a recording of a real unit (2026-11-06)

The owner's recordings of a real TR-707's factory patterns (`Research/707-echtes-device.wav`,
18.5 s, and a long one of 8.5 minutes, same source; not in the repository). To be taken with
care: nobody knows what was done to them. They are nearly mono (left and right correlate
0.9997), so they say nothing about pan, and they have a hard edge at 16 kHz, so they went
through a lossy codec. **measured** below means measured on these recordings. Scripts:
`tools/measure/tr707_hits.py`, `tr707_compare.py`, `tr707_hat_env.py`, `tr707_transfer.py`.

- **Samples:** not swapped. Every ROM sound is found in the recording (correlation up to
  0.96); the hats are the ROM's hat sample (0.8 above 4 kHz, about 1000 hits).
- **Hi-hat (measured, about 600 hits, recording over the ROM above 4 kHz):** the envelope was
  wrong, not the sample. Open hat: 0 dB (within 1 dB) for the whole sample, 300 ms. Closed
  hat: -2 dB at 20 ms, -6 at 40, -13 at 60, -22 at 80, -33 at 100, -45 at 120. MAME's linear
  VCA gave -12 at 20 ms and -25 at 40 (closed), -14 at 300 ms (open). Late in its decay the
  closed hat falls 0.6 dB per ms, a time constant of 14.5 ms: MAME's EG (14.2 ms) is right,
  the VCA is not, as MAME says itself. One curve fits both hats within 2 dB:
  gain = x / (1 + x), x = EG voltage / 0.18 V (`hatVca` in `Tr707.cpp`); a saturating
  single-transistor VCA, the 0.18 V a fit. An open hat's EG stays above the knee to the
  end of its sample. This replaces the check above for the closed hat's correlation; its
  render now lasts 137 ms to -60 dB.
- **Band passes (documented circuit, checked by measurement):** bass, snare and toms have
  a resistor and two anti-parallel diodes parallel to R1. MAME takes the diodes as always on.
  `DiodeBandpass` (`Analog.hpp`) simulates them (threshold 0.6 V, assumed): quiet signals
  see R1 alone. With it snare and toms follow the recording within 2-3 dB below 4 kHz, where
  they were up to 10 dB off.
- **Highs (measured, cause unknown):** recording over model, normalised at 0.8-2.5 kHz, a
  common curve on rim shot, cowbell, snare, hats and crash: +1.5 dB at 2.8 kHz, +3 at 3.6,
  +5 at 5.7, +7 at 7.1, +9 at 9, +11 at 11.3, +13 at 14.3. A single first-order zero at
  3.5 kHz. The documented circuit cannot rise (it has only falling RC poles), so either the
  recording was equalised or the unit has less droop than the schematic reads: this cannot
  be told without a unit at hand. Hence a control: **HIGHS** on the TR-707's page (the
  parameter `tr707_highs`), a shelf at the mix, zero 3.5 kHz, pole 40 kHz (`kHighsZero`,
  `kHighsPole`): 0 % is the circuit as documented, 100 % (the default, the owner's choice:
  the recording sounds right) is the recording. It applies to the mix. With it rim shot,
  cowbell and both hats follow the recording within 1.5 dB from 0.8 to 14 kHz.
- **Other envelopes, first reading (measured; obsolete for the multiplexed voices, see the
  next point):** bass drum, rim shot, clap within 2 dB, snare, tambourine, cowbell up to 3 dB
  (real slower) over the first 60 ms: left as they were. That came from a coherent gain that
  loses the signal after some 60 ms. Crash about -8 dB at 500 ms, -13 at 900 (model -9, -17),
  too few clean hits to change anything; ride unclear.
- **Envelopes of the multiplexed voices (measured, 2026-11-06, after the owner heard snare 1
  too short):** level per band over time, recording over ROM, hits with no hat after them
  (`tools/measure/tr707_envelopes.py`). Snare 1 and 2, cowbell, clap, tambourine and the toms
  follow their ROM data within 1-3 dB for as long as they can be told from the rest (snares
  120 ms, toms some 400 ms). MAME's EGs (0.047 uF: 103 or 220 ms) put the snare at -12 dB at
  100 ms, the toms at -18 at 400. The accent still has to act through these EGs, so they cannot
  saturate as the hat's VCA does: they must be slower. **0.47 uF** fits (`kMuxEgC`): ten times
  what MAME reads, not checked against a board. The sample does the decay, the EG the accent.
  The snare stays up to 4 dB under the recording late on, as the diode band pass loses gain
  with the level; velocity 64 is now -8.5 dB, 24 is -27 dB on snare 1 (-7, -22 before the
  diodes), not checked against a unit.
- **Open: the bass drum.** Recording over ROM: +8 to +14 dB at 280-560 Hz, -12 to -20 dB at
  1.7-4.5 kHz; the model +2 to +6 and -1 to -8. Bass drum 1 and 2 differ by 7 dB in the ROM in
  these bands and not in the recording, so it is either distortion of the fundamental or other
  sounds that always fall on the bass drum (a coherent average does not remove those).
  A grid over the input level, the diode threshold and the resistor found no fit (9.4 dB
  rms against 9.9 now). High tom above 5.7 kHz -7 to -9 dB, tambourine -5 dB: few clean hits.

## Accent over MIDI

- **documented** (TR-707 owner's manual): the ACCENT LEVEL knob "controls the intensity
  of the accent"; accents are written per step (weak, strong). The MIDI chart lists note on
  velocity as recognised (1-127) and sent as 40-124; nothing about the knob and MIDI.
- **derived** from the TR-727's OS (TR-727.md): a received note's accent is velocity / 2,
  straight to the accent DAC; the FF18 levels (a range per setting) are used only when the
  sequencer triggers. So the knob is taken to act on the sequencer's accents only, and the
  plugin has no accent knob (decided with the owner, 2026-11-04).
