# TR-727

## Sound ROMs

None are dumped as far as we know (obsolete since 2026-11-08: dumps exist, none is public;
see "Who has read them"). **documented** (MAME `roland_tr707.cpp`): the same
voice board as the TR-707 with other component values, and voice slot 2 clocked at 25 kHz
instead of 12.5 kHz. The code keeps a place for the images (roms/README.md).

### What is in the ROMs

**documented** (HKA Design's page on its TR-707 ROM Expansion, from its own dumps; the
chip assignment agrees with the service notes as quoted on the tr-707 list, message 239,
Feb 2006). IC34 and IC35 are one 64 KiB space of 8-bit linear PCM, in the TR-707's layout:

| ROM | Address | Length | Sound | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IC34 | $0000 even / $0001 odd | 4 KiB each | high bongo / low bongo | 25 kHz |
| IC34 | $2000 even / $2001 odd | 4 KiB each | muted / open high conga | 25 kHz |
| IC34 | $4000 | 8 KiB | low conga | 25 kHz |
| IC34 | $6000 | 8 KiB | high timbale | 12.5 kHz |
| IC35 | $0000 | 8 KiB | low timbale | 12.5 kHz |
| IC35 | $2000 | 8 KiB | whistle | 25 kHz |
| IC35 | $4000 even / $4001 odd | 4 KiB each | high agogo / low agogo | 25 kHz |
| IC35 | $6000 even / $6001 odd | 4 KiB each | cabasa / maracas | 25 kHz |

IC19 holds the quijada and IC22 the star chime, 6-bit linear PCM at 25 kHz, as the TR-707's
cymbals. **derived:** this agrees with the firmware's note tables below, the shared voices
named in the TR-8S data and MAME's 25 kHz on slot 2. The short and the long whistle are one
sample on the TR-707's closed and open hi-hat channels, so they differ only by envelope.

Part numbers: **documented** (MAME, `NO_DUMP` as of 2026-11-08) IC34 15179694, IC35
15179695, IC19 15179696, IC22 15179697, all HN61256P; the list's message 239 reads IC35 as
HN61256PC80 and the other three with the TR-707's PC numbers, which cannot hold for IC34,
IC19 and IC22, whose contents differ.

### Who has read them

From the archive of the Yahoo list tr-707, 2004-2019
(<https://www.florian-anwander.de/yahoo/tr-707/topic.html>, all 1060 mails searched, 2026-11-08):

- 2004-2005: chips swapped and stacked between machines, nobody reads one. Message 184
  (Sep 2005) still asks for someone to read a TR-727.
- Mar-Apr 2006: plutoniq9 (Ryan) pulls the ROMs of a TR-727 and gives up on reading them
  (307, 314): the pins are not JEDEC and the chip select must be clocked.
- Jan 2007: Nicholas Gregorich says he had read what seemed valid data from a TR-727's ROMs
  (446), over serial to a terminal. That is the only claim of a TR-727 reading on the list,
  and it does not say whether anything was kept or shared.
- May 2007: Thierry Eppherre uploads the TR-707's images to the list's files (540, 541;
  the source of the TR-707 dumps around today, most likely).
- Aug 2015: Richard Burnett asks for the TR-727's images and recalls them in the list's files
  (1378); no answer. Asked again later (1468), no answer. **The list's upload notices show
  TR-707 images only**, so he may have meant those; the notices are not complete (of the
  TR-707 folder only IC34&35 has one). Yahoo's files are gone, and the archive holds mails
  only.

So nothing on the list supports a dump around 2004. **documented:** HKA Design's TR-707 ROM
Expansion and Cymbals Board (hkadesign.org.uk) carry the stock sounds of both machines, read
from the ROMs of a real TR-727; no image is offered, only the hardware. Its method for reading
the chips: tell the programmer it is a 64 KiB ROM, put the chip select on A0, the other
address lines one higher, and drop every other byte. Pinout against a 27C256 (list, 310, not
checked): 12 of the 15 address lines moved, pin 26 chip select, pin 20 an address line, enable
active high; one of IC34 and IC35 has its chip select active high.

### The TR-8S update file (looked at 2026-11-08)

Whether the Boss/Roland TR-8S firmware holds the TR-727's samples: **not known**. Update
file `TR8S_UP.bin` (3 983 360 bytes, SHA-1 `b264647a274d12ef32f9ad40e44c2c0247ae47cd`; kept
out of the repository), a tar with two members:

- `rpg42_m0c0a_up.bin` (2 535 520 bytes): a plain 512-byte header (`App1_Main`, 2023/08/29,
  0.010001), then everything encrypted with a 64-bit block cipher in ECB mode (equal plaintext
  blocks give equal ciphertext blocks, on 8-byte boundaries only; other blocks share no more
  bytes than chance). Not decrypted, and the file itself shows no key. Whether samples are in
  here cannot be read off. **measured:** the TR-707 ROMs, as a whole or per voice slot, are not
  in it as contiguous 8-bit data under any byte mapping (the pattern of equal blocks would show
  it, where a slot has enough equal blocks to test). Resampled, compressed or cut-up data would
  not be seen.
- `rpg42_init_param.bin` (1 441 792 bytes): header `INIT`, then three identical copies, 465 275
  bytes each, LZSS (12-bit window, 4-bit length), 3 407 824 bytes decoded: kits and patterns,
  97 % zeros. No audio: none of 419 varied 16-byte probes of the TR-707 ROMs is found raw,
  offset by 128 or inverted (a few all-but-constant probes match, which is silence).

**read in the parameter data:** instrument names for all 15 TR-727 sounds. Names with a slash
name the partner of a shared voice, as the TR-707's names do for its known pairs (`707
Bass1/2`, `707 Snare1/2`, `707 Rim Shot/CB`, `707 Hand Clap/TB`, `707Tambourine/HC`):
`727 HighBongo/LB`, `727 LowBongo/HB`, `727MtHiConga/OHC`, `727OpHiConga/MHC`,
`727 HighAgogo/LA`, `727 Low Agogo/HA`, `727 Cabasa/MA`, `727 Maracas/CA`. **derived:** these
four pairs share a voice on the TR-727 the way BD1/2, SD1/2, RS/CB and HCP/TAMB do on the
TR-707. That agrees with the firmware's note table below, which puts them in exactly those
slots.

## Firmware

OS v1.0 (`osv_1.0_hd4827128.ic13`, 16 KiB, SHA-1
`8eb033d9729aa84cc3c33b8ce30925ff3c35e70a`, matches MAME). HD6303X; the ROM sits at
0x8000-0xBFFF and is mirrored at 0xC000-0xFFFF. Disassembled with MAME's `unidasm -arch
hd6301`.

- **firmware:** I/O: accent DAC at $5000, voice triggers at $6000 + mask, voice select at
  $7000.
- **firmware:** vectors: reset C000, SCI ED5B, IRQ2 ED4E, OCF E99A, TOF E992, ICF E719.
- **firmware:** the SCI interrupt fills a 32-byte ring buffer at $2F60 (write index $2F83,
  read index $2F82). It drops channel messages on other channels than the one at $2F00
  (`Bn` and `En` with their data bytes) and handles F8, FA, FB and FC itself. E371 and E37B
  read from the buffer.
- **firmware:** note tables, in instrument order BD1, SD1, LT, MT, HT, CH, RS, HCP, BD2,
  SD2, OH, CB, TAMB, CRASH, RIDE (the TR-707's names for the 15 voices):
  - FF97, the TR-707 map: 35, 38, 41, 45, 48, 42, 37, 39, 36, 40, 46, 56, 54, 49, 51
  - FFA6, the TR-727 map: 60, 62, 64, 65, 66, 71, 67, 69, 61, 63, 72, 68, 70, 73, 74

  Bit 0 of $BA chooses (set: the TR-707 map). Read by the MIDI out code at E26C.
- **documented:** the TR-727's voices on notes 60-74 (TR-727 manual, Sep 1985, p. 48, as
  tabulated in Zoe Blade's "Roland TR MIDI map"): 60 high bongo, 61 low bongo, 62 muted
  high conga, 63 open high conga, 64 low conga, 65 high timbale, 66 low timbale, 67 high
  agogo, 68 low agogo, 69 cabasa, 70 maracas, 71 short whistle, 72 long whistle, 73
  quijada, 74 star chime. Matches the receive map below (notes 60-74).
- **firmware:** two more MIDI-out tables of four notes each, FFB5 (40, 41, 45, 48, chosen
  in TR-707 mode) and FFB9 (63, 64, 65, 66, TR-727 mode), sent at E303 from four bits of
  $B7 with velocity $2F88 and switched off again eight ticks later (E348). What the four
  bits stand for is open; MIDI out is outside Ostrich 7765's scope.
- **firmware:** accent levels at FF18: 0x14-0x23 normal, 0x14-0x38 accented, used at DD12
  (index $9D, +0x10) when the sequencer triggers.

### MIDI receive

- **firmware:** the parser (E9DC-EAE2) runs only in one mode ($6D = 0x0C). It waits until a
  whole message is in the buffer and handles running status (a data byte reuses the last
  status, $C6). Note on: channel $2F00, or any channel when $2F01 is set (omni). Notes
  35-98 only. **Note off (8n) and note on with velocity 0 are ignored**: every voice plays
  to its end. Bn goes to E3E8/EAB0 (only 7C, omni on, seen so far), F2 and F3 to E3F8 and
  E3E8.
- **firmware:** after a reset (D93B) the channel is 10 ($2F00 = 9) and omni is off.
- **firmware:** the receive map is a 64-entry table at $2F10, one per note 35-98, holding
  a voice index; 0x10 or above means "no voice". The reset fills it with 0x10, then puts
  FF66 (0-11, 13, 14, 15) at notes 60-74: note 60 + i plays voice index FF66[i]. Voice
  index 12 is never used. A note can be assigned to a voice by hand (D2A8: the note last
  received, $C7, goes to the voice whose button is held).
- **firmware:** voice index n means trigger bit n (FF75: 1 << n, 16 bits). The note-on
  handler (E38F) ORs it into the pending mask $54/$55; the main loop takes the mask
  (DC8C, interrupts off) and clears it.
- **firmware: velocity to accent.** There is one accent DAC for all voices ($5000,
  6 bit). A MIDI note on stores velocity / 2 (0-63) in $C1; the main loop writes $C1 to
  the DAC (DD2C) before it fires the voices. So **all voices that start together share one
  accent: that of the last note received before they fire.** When voice 14 or 15 is
  pending (high byte of the mask & 0xC0), the accent is at least 15. A sequencer trigger
  uses the FF18 levels instead.

### Timing

- **documented** (MAME): HD6303X with a 4 MHz crystal, so the E clock and the timer run
  at 1 MHz.
- **firmware:** the output-compare interrupt (E99A) comes every 0x2BC = 700 timer counts,
  **0.7 ms**, and cycles through four phases ($86 = 0-3), each phase so every **2.8 ms**:
  - phase 3: parse every complete MIDI message in the buffer;
  - phase 0: fire the voices the main loop has prepared (write to $6000 + mask, voice
    select at $7000; EB9C);
  - phase 2: end the trigger pulses (EB83), so a trigger pulse lasts 1.4 ms;
  - phase 1: other work (EB19).
- **derived:** a MIDI note on therefore reaches the voices on a 2.8 ms grid. It waits
  0-2.8 ms for phase 3. If the main loop picks the mask up within the next 0.7 ms, the
  voice fires in phase 0; otherwise it fires one or more 2.8 ms rounds later. The main
  loop's period is not yet known (it varies with the mode and the display work). No
  TR-707 or TR-727 is at hand to measure it, so it must come from counting the main loop's
  cycles in the firmware.
- **Open:** the main loop's period, i.e. how often a note slips by one round.
